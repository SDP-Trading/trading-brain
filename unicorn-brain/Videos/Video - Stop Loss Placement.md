## LONG VERSION (YouTube — 8-10 min)

**Format:** horizontal, screen-share chart + camera in corner. Structure: hook → context → the 2 options → the wick case → real example → when to use each → close.

### [0:00–0:30] Intro / Hook
> "How many times have you placed a 'perfect' stop loss by the book, only for price to wick you out by two ticks and then run 5R in your direction? Today I'll show you exactly why that happens, and the two ways I place stops on a Unicorn setup — one tighter, one safer — and more importantly, when to use each one."

### [0:30–1:30] Context — the premise of the model
> "Before we talk about the stop, we need the premise. In the Unicorn model, the Breaker Block is what holds price up through the move — it's the zone that, if it holds, confirms the structure is still valid. That's why the 'default' stop location is below (or above, for shorts) the breaker: your invalidation point is literally the breaker failing."

*(Visual: pull up [[What is a Breaker Block]] — Bullish/Bearish diagram)*

### [1:30–3:00] The two options, explained
> "But in practice, there are two ways to approach this:"

*(Visual: bring up the [[Stop Loss Placement]] diagram — show both zones)*

1. **Below the Breaker Block** — the default recommendation. Tighter, better risk/reward if it works.
2. **Below the Low (or above the High) that swept liquidity** — the "safe" option. Wider, but gives you more room.

> "Look at the diagram: here's the low that took liquidity, then the breaker forms on the pullback, and price continues. The orange zone is the tight stop under the breaker. The pink zone, further down, is the stop under the full low."

### [3:00–5:00] The wick problem (the core point of this video)
> "So why not just always use the tighter option? Because price can — and often does — wick straight through the breaker, tag your stop, and still close the candle back above that level."

*(Visual: animate a wick piercing the orange zone while the candle body closes back above it)*

> "If your stop was sitting right there, you just got taken out of a trade that was technically still valid. Structure never broke — price just went to grab that leftover liquidity before continuing. That's why option two exists: stop under the full low. You're giving the trade room to survive that wick without invalidating something that was actually still working."

*(Note: if you have — or can pull — a real chart where this exact scenario played out for you, drop it in right here. This is the strongest beat in the video because it moves from theory to something the viewer can actually picture happening.)*

### [5:00–6:30] When to use each one
> "So which one do I use?"

- If the context is clean — strong displacement, well-formed breaker, minimal wicking on the impulse leg — I go with the tight stop, because the risk/reward improves quite a bit.
- If I'm seeing big wicks on the prior leg, or trading a more volatile session (news, open time), I'll take the stop under the full low — I'd rather accept a slightly worse R:R than get stopped out by noise.

*(Visual: optional — pull up 2 quick examples from [[Trade Examples]], one clean setup and one more volatile, to illustrate the difference)*

### [6:30–7:30] The common mistake
> "The mistake I see most often is people using the SAME option every single time, no matter the context — mechanically. That's exactly where the model stops working. It's not about which one is 'correct,' it's about adapting to what the chart is actually showing you in that moment."

### [7:30–8:30] Close / CTA
> "So next time you're not sure where to put your stop on a Unicorn setup, ask yourself: does this structure have big wicks behind it? If yes, give it more room. If it's clean, you can tighten the risk."
