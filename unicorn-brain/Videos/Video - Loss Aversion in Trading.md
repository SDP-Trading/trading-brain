## SHORT VERSION (Reels / TikTok / Shorts — ~48 sec)

**Format:** vertical, talking head. Fast cuts, no dead air.

**[0:00–0:08] HOOK (talking head, direct tone)**
> "You know when you close a winning trade way too early, but you hold onto a loser way longer than you should, hoping it turns around?"

**[0:08–0:20] The concept**
> "There's an actual concept behind that. It's called loss aversion — losing $100 feels about twice as bad as winning $100 feels good. Same amount of money, completely different reaction."

*(Visual: simple on-screen comparison — "-$100" with a bigger/heavier visual weight than "+$100")*

**[0:20–0:32] How it shows up in trading**
> "So because losing hits so much harder, you rush to lock in small wins before they can turn into losses. And you hold onto losers, because closing the trade is what makes the loss official."

*(Visual: split-screen or two quick chart cuts — a winner closed early vs. a loser held way past where it should've been cut)*

**[0:32–0:41] The fix**
> "You can't just will yourself out of it. What actually works is deciding your stop and your target before you're even in the trade, so you're not making that call in the moment."

*(Visual: cut to the risk/reward diagram — 1R stop, 2R target, both marked before entry)*

**[0:41–0:48] Close / CTA**
> "So next time you want to close a winner early, or you're telling yourself 'just a bit longer' on a loser — that's your brain reacting, not a real decision."

*(On-screen text: "Decide before you're in the trade, not during it." + CTA to your profile / indicator)*

### Production notes
- This one isn't built on a specific Unicorn Model concept note — it's trading psychology, and it exists to justify *why* the model's fixed rules matter (see [[Video - Why 2R is the Sweet Spot]] and [[Video - Consistency in Trading]]).
- "Loss aversion" (from behavioral economics) is the correct, specific term for this — "risk aversion" is the broader umbrella term it sits under. The "losing feels about twice as bad as winning feels good" framing is the standard way this finding gets cited in behavioral economics (Kahneman & Tversky's original work put the loss-aversion coefficient around 1.5–2.5x) — safe to say with confidence, though it's a general finding rather than something specific to trading.
- Strongest if you can reference a real trade of yours where you cut a winner too early or held a loser too long — even a quick "I've done this myself" beats keeping it fully general.
- Natural trilogy with [[Video - Consistency in Trading]] and [[Video - Why 2R is the Sweet Spot]] — all three make the same underlying point from different angles: fixed rules beat in-the-moment decisions.
