# Engineered Liquidity

⬆ [[Manipulation]]

See also: [[Time]]

One of the most effective ways to identify manipulation early on is by recognizing engineered liquidity.

Engineered liquidity occurs when market prices are deliberately manipulated to generate liquidity at specific price levels — for example, a series of equal highs or lows. Once this liquidity has been established, it's common to see a sharp price movement aimed at capturing it, which is often considered a sign of manipulation.

![[Engineered Liquidity.png]]

As covered in [[Time]], events that align with key time frames tend to carry more significance and increase the chances of success. Market manipulation, particularly through engineered liquidity, is no exception — these patterns often emerge just before critical time windows, such as session opens or news releases.
