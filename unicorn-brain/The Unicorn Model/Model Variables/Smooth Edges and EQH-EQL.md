Smooth Edges and draw on liquidity are among the model's non-mandatory (optional) variables. Having a draw on liquidity — especially in the form of Equal Highs/Lows or Smooth Edges — adds more rationale to a trade and provides a reason for price to move further.

Most of the time, Smooth Edges are used to establish context for price moving in a particular direction. If a Unicorn trade aligns with the direction of the Smooth Edges or Equal Highs/Lows, it's considered a higher-probability trade.

Smooth Edges are a formation of highs and lows where price establishes a high or low, and the preceding ones never surpass the previous one — this creates a singular, horizontal line extending from the first high (or low). The effectiveness of a Smooth Edges level increases the more highs/lows respect that horizontal formation, and a key confluence is the preservation of the first high/low along with subsequent intact highs/lows — an unbroken sequence adds reliability to the level.

![[Smooth Edges Diagram.png]]

Smooth Edges can form on any timeframe. However, when they occur within an important time window they provide greater confluence when applying these concepts. Typically, Smooth Edges will align with the Asia Range high/low, serving as a tier +A draw on liquidity to use as context for price movements.

This model uses a fixed 2R [[Take Profit]] by default — Smooth Edges become the reference point when looking to extend a trade beyond that.

See [[Smooth Edges as Draw on Liquidity]] for why price gravitates to these levels, and [[How to Spot Smooth Edges]] for where to look.
