# Swing High/Low Liquidity

Swing High and Swing Low liquidity refers to the orders resting above visible swing highs and below visible swing lows in the market.

A swing high is a price point where the market pushed up, rejected, and then moved lower.
A swing low is a price point where the market pushed down, rejected, and then moved higher.

These points are important because they are obvious reference levels for many traders. Retail traders often place stop losses, breakout orders, or pending orders around these areas. Because of this, swing highs and swing lows often become liquidity pools.

## How to use it

Swing liquidity gives us a possible area where price may react.

1. Identify the swing high or swing low.
2. Wait for price to trade into that level and take the liquidity.
3. After the liquidity is taken, drop to a lower timeframe and look for a clean entry model.

The goal is not to predict every sweep — it's to wait for liquidity to be taken and then look for a structured reason to enter.

- If price takes a previous **swing low** → look for bullish confirmation on a lower timeframe.
- If price takes a previous **swing high** → look for bearish confirmation on a lower timeframe.

The sweep gives the context. The lower timeframe gives the entry.
