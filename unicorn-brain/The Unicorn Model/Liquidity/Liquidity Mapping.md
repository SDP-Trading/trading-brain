Understanding where liquidity is located is only the first step. The real value comes from knowing how to use it in a trading plan.

Usually, when price takes liquidity, the market reacts because the orders resting in that area are being triggered. This reaction is what gives us a potential opportunity to start participating in the market.

However, we do not enter simply because liquidity has been taken. We need a structured way to connect liquidity areas with actionable entries. This is where Liquidity Mapping comes in.

![[Liquidity Mapping - Reaction.png]]

Liquidity Mapping is the process of pairing a higher timeframe liquidity area with a lower timeframe entry model.

In other words, when price reaches or takes liquidity on a higher timeframe, we then drop to a lower timeframe to look for a precise entry setup.

The higher timeframe gives us the liquidity objective. The lower timeframe gives us the execution.

For example, if price takes a key liquidity level on the 1-hour chart, we may then look for an entry on the 5-minute chart. This allows us to use the higher timeframe for context while using the lower timeframe for better precision, risk control, and trade execution.
