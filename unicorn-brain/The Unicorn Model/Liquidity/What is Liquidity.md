# What is Liquidity

⬆ [[Liquidity]]

See also: [[Liquidity Mapping]] · [[Swing High-Low Liquidity]]

Liquidity refers to areas in the market where orders are likely to be resting. These areas are commonly found above previous highs and below previous lows, because many traders place stop losses, breakout orders, or pending orders around those levels.

When price moves toward liquidity, it is often moving into an area where there are enough orders available to be executed. This matters because larger market participants need liquidity to enter or exit positions efficiently, without causing unnecessary price disruption.

In simple terms, liquidity acts as a target for price. The market often moves toward obvious highs or lows to access the orders resting there before either continuing in the same direction or reversing.

Buy-side liquidity is found above highs. Sell-side liquidity is found below lows.

A simple way to remember it: liquidity is where orders are waiting, and price often moves there to fill them.
