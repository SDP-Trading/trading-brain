# Futures 101 — Course Structure

A beginner course for someone who has never traded futures before. This sits **before** [[Course Structure]] (The Unicorn Model course) — it teaches the mechanics and vocabulary the Unicorn course assumes you already have (contracts, margin, chart basics, order types, session hours) so a complete beginner isn't lost the moment liquidity or timeframes come up.

None of this content exists in the vault yet — every lesson below is new material to write, not pulled from an existing note.

**Status per lesson:**
- 🆕 To write — outline only, content not drafted yet
- 🟡 Drafted — written, not reviewed
- ✅ Ready

## Module 1 — What Is a Futures Contract?

- [ ] 🆕 What a futures contract actually is — a standardized agreement to buy/sell an asset at a set price on a future date, and why that matters for how you trade it (expiration, contract months, rollover)
- [ ] 🆕 Futures vs. stocks vs. options — no share ownership, built-in leverage, no pattern day trader rule, near 24-hour market
- [ ] 🆕 Why traders choose futures — liquidity, capital efficiency, extended hours, and the tradeoffs that come with each

## Module 2 — Contract Specs & Pricing

- [ ] 🆕 Tick size and tick value — what a "tick" is and how it translates into dollars (worked example: ES = 0.25 pts = $12.50/tick)
- [ ] 🆕 Contract multiplier / point value — how P&L is actually calculated
- [ ] 🆕 Micro vs. mini contracts — MES/MNQ vs. ES/NQ, and why micros exist for smaller accounts
- [ ] 🆕 The contracts you'll see most — ES, NQ, CL, GC, 6E — what each one tracks and who tends to trade it

## Module 3 — Margin, Leverage & Getting an Account

- [ ] 🆕 Initial margin vs. maintenance margin — what you need to open and hold a position
- [ ] 🆕 Day trading margin vs. overnight margin — why the number changes if you hold past the close
- [ ] 🆕 What a margin call is and how to avoid one
- [ ] 🆕 Choosing a broker/platform — criteria to evaluate (regulation, commissions, data feed, platform), not a specific recommendation
- [ ] 🆕 Funded / prop trading accounts as an alternative path — how they work, and how they differ from a personal account

## Module 4 — Reading a Chart

- [ ] 🆕 Candlestick basics — open, high, low, close, body vs. wick
- [ ] 🆕 Timeframes — what they are and how picking one changes what you see
- [ ] 🆕 Volume basics — what it tells you, what it doesn't
- [ ] 🆕 Support and resistance — the beginner version of what later becomes "liquidity" in the Unicorn Model

## Module 5 — Order Types & Execution

- [ ] 🆕 Market order — instant fill, no price control
- [ ] 🆕 Limit order — price control, no fill guarantee
- [ ] 🆕 Stop order / stop-loss — how it protects you, and how it can still slip in fast markets
- [ ] 🆕 Stop-limit order — the hybrid, and its main risk (no fill at all)
- [ ] 🆕 OCO (one-cancels-other) brackets — setting your stop and target together
- [ ] 🆕 Walkthrough: placing a full trade end-to-end on a platform

## Module 6 — Trading Sessions & Market Hours

- [ ] 🆕 Futures trade nearly 24 hours — what that actually means day to day
- [ ] 🆕 The three main sessions — Asia, London, New York — and how volatility differs between them
- [ ] 🆕 Why session choice matters even before you have a strategy

*(This module is the natural bridge into [[Time]] in the Unicorn course — same three sessions, revisited with manipulation and timing layered on top.)*

## Module 7 — Risk Management Fundamentals

- [ ] 🆕 Position sizing — contracts relative to account size, not "how many can I afford"
- [ ] 🆕 What "R" means and why thinking in R multiples beats thinking in dollars
- [ ] 🆕 Setting a max daily/weekly loss limit before you ever place a trade
- [ ] 🆕 Why risk management matters more than being right on any single trade

## Module 8 — Common Beginner Mistakes

- [ ] 🆕 Overleveraging — trading more contracts than the account supports
- [ ] 🆕 No stop loss, or moving it after the trade is already open
- [ ] 🆕 Revenge trading after a loss
- [ ] 🆕 Trading without a plan or a journal
- [ ] 🆕 Overtrading — taking every session instead of the ones that fit your plan

## Module 9 — Your Trading Plan & Next Steps

- [ ] 🆕 What a trading plan should actually contain — entry criteria, risk per trade, daily loss limit, sessions you trade
- [ ] 🆕 Keeping a trade journal — what to log and why it's the fastest way to improve
- [ ] 🆕 Where to go from here — once these fundamentals are solid, [[Course Structure|The Unicorn Model course]] is the next step

## Notes on scope

This course intentionally stays neutral and mechanical — no specific broker endorsement, no promise of results, no strategy of its own. Its only job is to remove the vocabulary and mechanics gap so a complete beginner can walk into the Unicorn Model course without getting stuck on "what's a tick" or "what's margin."

*Educational content only — not financial advice. Futures trading carries substantial risk, including the risk of loss beyond your initial deposit.*
